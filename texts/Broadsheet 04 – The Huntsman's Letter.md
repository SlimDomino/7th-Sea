# The Altamira Broadsheets – No. 4: The Huntsman's Letter

Date: printed Julius 11, 1669
Location: sold in the plazas of Altamira for a centimo. Believe at your own risk.


## La Gaceta de Altamira

*Licensed by the Holy Church*

### The Bells of Vaticine City

**After Three Years, Holy Church Has a Hierophant**

The Gaceta rejoices to report that the College of Cardinals, gathered in Vaticine City, has at long last elected a new Hierophant. The bells of every church in Altamira were rung in thanksgiving.

The Gaceta is told that the cardinals could not agree for a very long time, until they found a man whom none of them disliked. His name and the first letters of his reign will be printed here as soon as the official correspondence arrives. The faithful are reminded that the Holy Office of the Inquisition continues its work in Castille as before.

The Gaceta also notes, with the greatest caution, a rumor from Montaigne that the missing archbishops of that country have been found alive. The Gaceta does not print rumors. It merely reports that this one is being repeated.

With the licence of the Most Illustrious Bishop of Altamira.


## El Pregonero

*The Town Crier of Altamira*

### PIRATES STORM QUINCEAÑERA!!

**Fireworks! Dancing Sailors! A Man Without a Shirt! Iron Giant Strikes Again – This Time with a Violin!**

> *[Woodcut: four men in matching costumes leaping through a burst of fireworks into a crowded courtyard, one of them dressed as a clown and one of them, regrettably, without a shirt.]*

Polite society was SHAKEN TO ITS FOUNDATIONS on the evening of the tenth, when the quinceañera of Doña María José Torres y Soldano at the Hacienda de los Robles was INVADED by sailors from THE STRIPED SHIP.

There were four of them: the prodigal son of the house, THE IRON-FISTED GIANT, a sailor dressed as A CLOWN, and a fourth man who, in the course of a wild dance before the assembled nobility of the province, REMOVED HIS SHIRT. Fireworks went off behind them as they entered. Guests report that the dancing was "very well rehearsed." El Pregonero asks: rehearsed FOR WHAT?

Then the giant presented the birthday girl with A VIOLIN. A violin worth more than a house, our sources say, carried into a house of mourning by a man last seen hurling gold at butchers. Where does a sailor get such money? El Pregonero does not speculate. El Pregonero merely asks.

The guests were heard to ask one another whether the crew of the striped ship are all thieves. El Pregonero hears that the giant overheard, and was NOT PLEASED.


## La Lengua de Altamira

*Society, Scandal and Whispers*

### From Behind the Fan

**Fifteen Candles at Las Robles**

What a night! Doña Isabel insisted the party go ahead despite poor Don Juan, and how right she was. Ribbons and garlands on every balcony, a hundred lanterns, and more wine than the province could drink in a week. Black ribbons were tied among the bright ones, every member of the family wore black on one sleeve, and one place at the head table was left empty, with a single white candle. Your columnist was not the only one who wept.

The birthday girl wore blue and cream and looked, everyone said, exactly like her mother. Don Emilio climbed onto his chair to toast her: "The cleverest person in this house, and she knows it, and she has the good manners never to say so." He then danced the first dance with her, and he is a far better dancer than any of us suspected.

Ambitious mothers, take note: there were no suitors. Don Emilio let it be known that this year his daughter dances only with her family. Her brothers obliged. Don Cristóbal danced as he fences. Father Pedro was astonishingly light on his feet. And the prodigal brother danced a flamenco with her, wearing a cloak of peacock feathers, and gave her a matching one.

Your columnist is told that the same brother also gave her a pair of pistols. Your columnist hopes this is not true. Your columnist suspects that it is.

And the foreign captain's violin? A Luckau, if you please, from the captain's own village, where the old maker turns out perhaps one a year. Doña María José thanked him very prettily. Whether she can play it, Altamira has yet to discover.

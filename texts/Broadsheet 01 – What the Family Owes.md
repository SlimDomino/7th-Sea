# The Altamira Broadsheets – No. 1: What the Family Owes

Date: printed Julius 6, 1669, the first morning of Los Borrachos
Location: sold in the plazas of Altamira for a centimo. Believe at your own risk.


## La Gaceta de Altamira

*Licensed by the Holy Church*

### A Son of Las Robles Laid to Rest

**Don Juan Torres y Soldano Commended to Theus Before a Multitude of Mourners**

On the fifth of Julius, in the family chapel of the Hacienda de los Robles, the earthly remains of Don Juan Torres y Soldano, eldest son of Don Emilio and Doña Isabel, were committed to the ground with every rite of Holy Mother Church.

The Mass was said by the young man's own brother, Father Pedro Torres y Soldano, whose composure was an example to all present. In his homily he spoke of a brother who "gave his life the way he lived it, standing between the people he loved and whatever came for them," words the Gaceta prints here for the edification of its readers.

The Gaceta is informed that Don Juan's death was sudden. It does not inquire further, and trusts its readers will show the family the same Christian courtesy.

With the licence of the Most Illustrious Bishop of Altamira.


## El Pregonero

*The Town Crier of Altamira*

### THE PRODIGAL RETURNS – WITH A PIRATE IN TOW!

**Long-Lost Son of Las Robles Home After Seven Years at Sea! Brings Iron-Handed Foreigner to His Brother's Deathbed! Striped Ship of Mystery in Our Harbor!**

> *[Woodcut: a ragged sailor kneeling at a deathbed while a towering figure with a hook for a hand looms in the doorway.]*

SEVEN YEARS! That is how long the seventh son of Don Emilio Torres y Soldano has been gone from this province, having fled a seminary (El Pregonero has this from a reliable source) and run away to sea. Now he is home, in time to see his brother into the grave, and he did not come home alone.

His companion is AN EISEN SEA CAPTAIN, taller than a doorway, with a right hand made entirely of BLACK IRON. Servants at the hacienda report that the foreigner was found wandering the house after dark, lost and pale, and that he was later seen around the corner of the building in a state this paper will not describe to families.

And in the harbor, readers may have marked a ship like no other: RED AND WHITE STRIPED SAILS, and on her deck, at all hours, ACROBATS, JUGGLERS and A MAN EATING FIRE. Is she a ship or a circus? El Pregonero intends to find out.


## La Lengua de Altamira

*Society, Scandal and Whispers*

### From Behind the Fan

**Black Lace and Long Faces at Las Robles**

Half of Altamira squeezed into the chapel at Las Robles for poor Don Juan, and your columnist was there to see who wept and who did not.

Doña Isabel's mourning was exactly correct, as it always is. Don Emilio greeted every guest by name, as he always does. Don Hernando stood as if he might shatter, and Don Cristóbal, usually the steadiest blade in the province, was white as chalk and flinched every time Father Pedro raised his voice. Grief takes us all differently.

The youngest, little Doña María José, did not shed a tear in public. Your columnist notes it only because she was seen to watch everyone else who did.

And the prodigal brother, lately of the sea, stood at the front with the face of a man carrying a great deal more than grief. Altamira wonders what. Altamira always wonders.

Tomorrow, Los Borrachos. The family, we are told, will be seen in town as usual. Life goes on.

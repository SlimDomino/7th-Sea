LA GACETA DE ALTAMIRA
Licensed by the Holy Church
================================================================

MARTYRDOM IN THE WAREHOUSE QUARTER
Faithful Servants of the Holy Office Set Upon by Foreign
Brigands on the Blessed First Night of Los Borrachos

On the evening of the sixth of Julius, as the good people of
Altamira gave thanks with fireworks and song, a company of the
Holy Office's faithful brothers, engaged in their customary and
peaceable patrol of the warehouse quarter, was set upon without
provocation by a band of heretical cutthroats.

The ringleader, by the testimony of several brothers who
survived the assault, was an Eisen of monstrous size, a known
follower of the Objectionist heresy, whose right hand has been
replaced by an engine of iron in the manner of the godless
northern barbarians. His accomplice, a man of dissolute habits
who may be Castillian by birth but is certainly no Castillian in
spirit, was seen to fly through the air from the rooftops "like
a bat out of the Abyss."

The brigands were joined in the attack by a mob of armed sailors
from a vessel of the lowest character, which the Gaceta will not
dignify by naming. The officer commanding the patrol was most
grievously wounded and robbed of a sacred relic, a precious stone
of great holiness entrusted to him by his superiors.

The Holy Office assures the faithful that the guilty are known,
that their descriptions have been recorded, and that justice is
patient. Any person with knowledge of a tall Eisen with an iron
hand is asked to present himself at the offices of the Holy
Inquisition, where his discretion will be rewarded.

With the licence of the Most Illustrious Bishop of Altamira.

EL PREGONERO
The Town Crier of Altamira
================================================================

THIRTY INQUISITORS SLAIN BY IRON-FISTED GIANT!!
Carnival of Thieves Drops Anchor in Our Harbor!
Flying Man Seen Over Rooftops! Fireworks Were Cover for
Massacre, Say Witnesses!

[Woodcut: an enormous bearded man, at least nine feet tall,
holding a priest over his head with an iron claw, while a man
with bat wings swoops overhead and a striped ship full of
jugglers fires cannons in the background.]

ALTAMIRA REELS! As our fair city lifted its eyes to the
fireworks on the first night of Los Borrachos, a horror was
unfolding in the warehouse streets that this paper can only
describe as A MASSACRE.

Thirty of the Holy Office's bravest (some say forty) were cut
down in under a minute by a giant from the frozen north, a man
with a fist of black iron that, our eyewitness swears, "glowed
red like a forge and tore through steel as if it were bread." He
was assisted by a second villain who leapt from a rooftop, turned
three somersaults in the air, and killed seven men before his
boots touched the cobbles.

THE CARNIVAL SHIP! Readers will have seen the vessel with the
striped sails now moored at our docks, its decks crowded with
acrobats, fire-eaters and a captain dressed as a clown. El
Pregonero has learned that this "carnival" is nothing of the
sort. When the slaughter began, its crew poured down the gangplank
armed to the teeth, as if they had been waiting for the signal. A
respected dockside merchant tells us, "My nephew says the whole
crew are thieves, and my nephew would know."

THE CURSED STONE! The wounded officer was robbed of a jewel which,
our sources whisper, was RED AS BLOOD and WARM TO THE TOUCH. What
could a foreign giant want with such a thing? El Pregonero does
not speculate. El Pregonero merely asks.

Mothers, keep your daughters indoors! Merchants, count your
silver! And if you see a tall Eisen in black and gold, CROSS THE
STREET!

LA LENGUA DE ALTAMIRA
Society, Scandal and Whispers
================================================================

FROM BEHIND THE FAN
A Family Quarrel on the Old Bridge

Who were the two young gentlemen who stopped all traffic on the
old stone bridge at dusk on the sixth, rolling about in the dirt
among the wine barrels, weeping and shouting about "lies" and
"brotherhood"? Our correspondent, who was three carts back, heard
one accuse the other of having lied to him for years about being
Castillian. A lovers' quarrel, say some. A gambling debt, say
others. Your columnist notes only that one of the gentlemen bore
a striking likeness to a certain prodigal son of a certain great
house on the Las Robles road, lately returned from the sea for a
funeral, and that his companion was very tall, very foreign, and
very, very handsome.

Meanwhile, the same evening, a certain young lady of that same
great house, only days from her fifteenth birthday, was seen
passing the bridge in deepest mourning on her way to call on a
sea captain, accompanied by a widowed cousin nobody in Altamira
society has ever heard of. A Doña Beatriz de Salcedo. Of the
Salcedos of...? Your columnist confesses she cannot place the
family. Can any reader?

And what became of the drunken young cousin who rode with the
quarreling gentlemen? A slip of a boy called "Rodrigo," so the
bridge sergeant tells us, who was never seen again after the
fireworks. Altamira wonders. Altamira always wonders.

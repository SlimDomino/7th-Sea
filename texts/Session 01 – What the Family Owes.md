# Session 01 – What the Family Owes

Played: Tuesday, October 6, 2026 (about 1 hour 27 minutes, on Discord)
In-game: the evening of Sextus 29, 1669, through the afternoon of Julius 5, 1669
Place: Hacienda de los Robles, near Altamira, Castille

Cast
- Rafi (Rafael) Torres y Soldano – Joey
- Captain Lute – Jessie
- María José Soldano y Torres – Melissa (who also voiced Doña Isabel tonight)
- GM – Gabriel (Don Emilio, Tomás, Father Pedro and everyone else)


## Previously

Rafi came home to Las Robles in time to watch his brother Juan die. From Cristóbal and Hernando he dragged out the story of the fight: the Inquisitors, Juan's order, Cristóbal's blade, the bodies Hernando buried. What he still didn't know was why a family of horse ranchers was hiding a fugitive from the Inquisition in the first place. Everyone kept telling him to go ask his parents.


## The Study

Evening, Sextus 29. Rafi walks into his father's study: the same maps on the walls, the same smell of pipe smoke and leather, the same heavy desk he used to hide under as a boy. Someone has set out good brandy and three glasses, which tells him they knew he was coming before he did. Don Emilio reaches for his easy, practiced smile before remembering what day it is. Doña Isabel, already in black, looks at her son the way she looks at a horse she hasn't made up her mind about, and nods at the empty chair.

Emilio starts on the gentle version. Juan was brave, the Church isn't the Church of their fathers, his brother died doing a hard thing but a good one. Isabel cuts him off: "He did not come here to be comforted. Tell him, or I will."

Then she tells him herself. Los Vagos. The family has been part of it since before Rafi was born, since before she married Emilio. The horses are real and the money is real. "Everything people say about Las Robles is true. It is just not all of it."

The bedtime stories were true. Los Vagos isn't one masked man in black. It's an organization of people sworn to protect the young King Sandoval and to protect common folk from cruel nobles. Lately it has taken on a third cause: shielding Castille from an Inquisition with no Hierophant above it, burning people on the flimsiest excuses. The horses are how things move up and down the coast without anyone looking twice. Father Pedro's "church business" is where the money is cleaned and passed along.

Then the gut punch. They didn't send Rafi to seminary because they didn't know what to do with him. They sent him because they did. A priest can go anywhere and hears everything, and nobody searches a priest's saddlebags. That was meant to be Rafi's road. Tomás is walking it now, and Emilio says Rafi would have been better at it. They meant to tell him once he'd taken his vows, but he was gone.

Isabel gives him something he never expected: an admission. Letting him leave believing nobody wanted him was her decision, not Emilio's. His father wanted to tell him at fifteen and she said he wasn't ready. "I was wrong about that. And I am not wrong very often." She won't apologize for what the family has done. She'd do all of it again, and so would Juan, who chose it the same as the rest of them and chose it again in that courtyard.

Rafi came in ready for a fight and the fight never came. He asks for something he can do, a target, the men behind his brother's killers. Emilio's answer is simpler and harder. First they bury Juan. After the funeral comes Los Borrachos, and the whole town will be watching. Rafi has to wear his face like a mask, the way his father always has. There's a place for him in the family and in Los Vagos if he wants it. But two churchmen came to this house and never left it, someone will notice, and someone will come. Emilio tells him to talk to Tomás, then decide for himself: "You left us a boy and you have come back to us a man. Do not do it for us. Do it for yourself."

As Rafi leaves, Emilio downs Rafi's untouched brandy and turns to his wife.


## Meanwhile, Captain Lute

While the family fell apart and pulled itself back together, Captain Lute woke up on a very expensive couch in a house he didn't know, after dark, badly regretting the salsa. A lost, slightly panicked tour of the parlor, the dining room and the great hall ended with him outside, around the corner of the house, alone with the night and his dignity. He survived.


## Partners

Rafi finds his captain on the grounds and unloads everything: everything he believed about his family was wrong. Lute's first worry is the obvious one. Is Rafi staying ashore while Lute sails off without him?

Rafi won't abandon his family, but he isn't the type to be kept in one place either, and he needs his friend. Lute agrees to stay and see it through, with one condition: if Rafi gets to be Zorro, Lute gets a bandit name of his own. He'll be **Dusk**. And they're partners, not hero and sidekick. They shake on it. Lute also wants to check on the ship and see what funds they're working with, and Rafi figures he's owed about fifteen years of back allowance.


## The Funeral

Julius 5, almost a week after Juan's death. A bright, merciless Castilian morning. The family chapel is full past the doors with neighbors, tenants and what looks like half of Altamira, all there for a young man everyone loved in their own way.

Rafi stands at the front carrying what he now knows. He watches his father greet mourners with practiced warmth and his mother arrange her face into exactly the right grief, and understands for the first time that he's watching two people who have spent decades doing what he's now being asked to do. Hernando stands like something brittle. Cristóbal is paler than Rafi has ever seen him, knuckles white, flinching every time the priest's voice rises.

The priest is Father Pedro, his own brother: spotless vestments, perfectly steady, the family's quiet, boring third son. Now Rafi knows what moves through those ledgers, and that this place at the altar was the one set aside for him. Pedro's voice catches only once, when he says Juan "gave his life the way he lived it, standing between the people he loved and whatever came for them." Half the chapel hears a figure of speech. Rafi hears a confession made in plain sight, and he suspects Cristóbal does too. When Rafi passes the casket, Pedro's eyes meet his for a heartbeat. No surprise in them. He knows Rafi knows.

At the casket, everything else falls away and leaves only Juan, gone. *There are other worlds than these.* Rafi still doesn't know what his brother meant. At the burial Pedro casts the first earth, and his hand stays closed a moment too long before he lets it fall. Ana stands beside Rafi the whole time, saying nothing, and somehow that's enough.

María José finds Rafi before their father does. She doesn't cry, though her eyes say she did earlier, somewhere private. She slips her hand into his for a moment. "You look like you're carrying something." Not a question. "That's all right. You don't have to tell me. I already know more than anyone thinks I do." Her eyes flick once toward Pedro, and something almost like a smile crosses her face. "I usually do." Then she drifts back to Ana's side and leaves her brother wondering what his fourteen-year-old sister just told him.

Emilio finds him next. Tomorrow the town holds Los Borrachos, a week of drinking, dancing, parades and fireworks, and the family is expected to be seen there. "I know how that sounds, today of all days. But some obligations don't wait for grief to finish."


## Tomás's Favor

Under the oaks the house is named for, Tomás finds Rafi at the edge of the thinning crowd. He's leaning on a walking stick he plainly resents and wearing borrowed black with sleeves too long. He's sorry he wasn't there for Juan at the end. Then he sees Rafi's face: they told you.

Juan wasn't discovered. The fugitive he was sheltering was followed to Las Robles. And the coded letter Rafi carried to Old Pico without knowing what it was? That was Tomás's mission. It was a warning that the Inquisition was about to raid another chapter. Eleven people were gone before the raid came through their door, and the Inquisitors found an empty building. Those eleven are alive because Rafi did the job right without even knowing what he was carrying.

Now Tomás needs another favor. The guest who has been sheltering at Las Robles, hidden in a house full of strange faces, was supposed to be his to deliver. He was going to ride her into Altamira himself, until a horse put him in a ditch. A ship in Altamira sails on the night tide the day after tomorrow, and her captain is one of theirs. If the woman is aboard, she lives. If she isn't, she burns.

Tomorrow night the whole town will be drunk, singing and watching fireworks. Nobody looks twice at a few riders during Los Borrachos, and it's the best cover they'll get all year. The trip is overland to the Altamira docks, where Rafi's own ship is berthed. Expect Inquisition checkpoints and patrols in the city. Tomás can't ride hard and can't run, and if anything goes wrong on that road she needs someone who can do both.

Rafi takes the job. Complications, he says, can be handled between Captain Lute and himself.


## Loose Threads

- **The escort.** Get Tomás's fugitive from Las Robles to the Altamira docks during Los Borrachos, past checkpoints and patrols, before the night-tide sailing.
- **Rafi's choice.** He's been offered a place in Los Vagos and told to talk with Tomás and decide for himself, for his own reasons.
- **"Someone will come."** Two Inquisitors walked into Las Robles and never walked out. Sooner or later someone will notice.
- **Pedro.** He knows Rafi knows. Neither has said a word.
- **María José.** "I already know more than anyone thinks I do." What does she know, and how?
- **Cristóbal.** He heard the confession in Pedro's eulogy too, and he isn't coping.
- **Lute's affairs.** Check on the ship and its funds. And Dusk is going to need a mask.

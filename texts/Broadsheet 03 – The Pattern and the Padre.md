# The Altamira Broadsheets – No. 3: The Pattern and the Padre

Date: printed Julius 9, 1669, in the last days of Los Borrachos
Location: sold in the plazas of Altamira for a centimo. Believe at your own risk.


## La Gaceta de Altamira

*Licensed by the Holy Church*

### A Notice from the Bishop's Palace

**Padre Baltasar Ruiz de Olmedo Called to Penance at San Bruno**

By order of His Excellency the Bishop of Altamira, the Reverend Padre Baltasar Ruiz de Olmedo is directed to present himself at the monastery of San Bruno, there to observe five years of silent penance and reflection, for the good of his soul and the peace of this diocese.

The Gaceta is informed that the Padre has requested, and been granted, a short delay in order to settle his affairs. It trusts that he will present himself at San Bruno at the appointed time, and it reminds the faithful that the sacraments are holy and are not administered from fountains.

The Gaceta further reports, with regret, that the Montaigne trade in Altamira continues despite the war. A Montaigne dealer in fine goods complains of having been called, in a foreign tongue, a name the Gaceta will not print. The Gaceta suggests the dealer consider whether he ought to be in Altamira at all.

With the licence of the Most Illustrious Bishop of Altamira.


## El Pregonero

*The Town Crier of Altamira*

### PRIEST IN FOUNTAIN! BUTCHERS IN PURSUIT! IRON GIANT CARRIES HIM OFF!

**Drunken Padre Baptizes the Fish Market! Five Butchers Demand Blood! Eisen Brute Seizes Holy Man, Hurls Gold at Mob!**

> *[Woodcut: a fat priest standing in a fountain, flinging water from his hat at a cheering crowd, while five men with cleavers charge in from one side and a giant with an iron claw drags the priest off by the collar.]*

SCANDAL IN THE FISH MARKET SQUARE! Before a crowd of hundreds, a priest of Holy Church, knee-deep in the great stone fountain with a bottle of wine in one hand, was seen BLESSING THE CROWD with water scooped up in his hat. Women swooned. Men threw coins. Several ladies, our correspondent observes, laughed far harder than the occasion called for.

Then came THE BUTCHERS. Five of them, in bloody aprons, the foremost WEEPING. The priest, they cried, had been found IN A BUTCHER'S HOUSE, IN HIS WIFE'S ROOM, AT MIDNIGHT. The priest, unashamed, declared that he had only been hearing her confession.

Murder was in the air when out of the crowd stepped none other than THE IRON-FISTED GIANT of the warehouse massacre. He took the priest by the collar, roared at him in the barbarous northern tongue, and dragged him away, flinging a fistful of gold over his shoulder. The butchers, El Pregonero is sorry to report, stopped to pick it up.

Where did the giant take the priest? El Pregonero hears it was ONTO THE STRIPED SHIP. A carnival ship. With a priest aboard. Readers may draw their own conclusions.


## La Lengua de Altamira

*Society, Scandal and Whispers*

### From Behind the Fan

**The Foreign Captain Goes Shopping**

Altamira's shopkeepers have a new favorite customer, and he is very tall, very foreign, and does not know how to count.

The Eisen captain from the striped ship has been seen all over town this week. He bought a violin from a Montaigne dealer on the Calle de los Plateros for eight hundred guilders without haggling once, and stormed out without a word of thanks (or rather, with several words, in Eisen, which the dealer is still trying to have translated). He very nearly bought a Montaigne porcelain doll as well. Your columnist shudders.

He has also commissioned two suits of clothes from a seamstress near the cathedral, one of them, she confides, "like a mariachi who has been to a funeral," and paid her twice what she asked. Then he came back an hour later and paid her again.

For whom is the violin? Your columnist notes that a certain young lady of a certain great house on the Las Robles road turns fifteen on the tenth. Your columnist notes nothing else. Your columnist merely notes.

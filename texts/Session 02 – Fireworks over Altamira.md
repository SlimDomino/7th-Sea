# Session 02 – Fireworks over Altamira

Played: Monday, October 12, 2026 (about 3 hours 15 minutes, on Discord)
In-game: the evening of Julius 5, 1669, through the first night of Los Borrachos, Julius 6
Place: Hacienda de los Robles, the road into Altamira, and the Altamira warehouse district

Cast
- Rafi (Rafael) Torres y Soldano – Joey
- Captain Lute – Jessie
- María José Soldano y Torres – Melissa
- GM – Gabriel (Inés, Sancho, the Inquisitors, Lute's crew and everyone else)


## Previously

Juan is buried. Rafi knows the truth about Los Vagos, and Tomás has handed him a job: get the fugitive who has been hiding at Las Robles to a ship in Altamira before it sails on the night tide, using the first night of Los Borrachos as cover. Expect checkpoints. Expect patrols.


## María José's Offer

Late afternoon after the funeral, as the household drifts apart, María José slips her arm through Captain Lute's and asks him to walk with her. She has heard, she says, that someone may need help getting somewhere safely tomorrow night, and she may be able to provide the means.

Lute, gallant and badly misreading the moment, assures her that his heart is already taken, though he would gladly carry her to the docks. María José goes scarlet. No, no, no, that is not what she meant at all. She is much too young for thoughts like that. She only assumed he had already been asked, and she'd like him to remember her offer when he is. They walk back to the others with as much dignity as either can manage.

Lute tells Rafi. Rafi tells Lute to stop flirting with his little sister. Lute insists he has a girlfriend. Then María José finds them both and lays out her plan.


## The Plan

She has a reason to be in Altamira tomorrow evening that nobody will question. Captain Ocaña, who does a great deal of business with the family, is in port, and with Mama and Papa occupied someone from Las Robles ought to call on him. A girl of good family doesn't travel alone. She will go with a female chaperone, a respectable widow, and a man from the hacienda as her protector.

The chaperone is the fugitive, traveling openly as a woman in widow's black under a distinctive lace mantilla. At the ship a woman is already waiting, dressed in black. The two swap headpieces, and María José walks back down the gangplank with what looks like the same woman she walked up with. The fugitive stays aboard and sails. Meanwhile Rafi and Lute escort someone else entirely, and draw every eye they can.

One condition: María José doesn't want to know the fugitive's real name. The less she knows, the less she can tell.

The pieces:

- **The fugitive** travels as **Doña Beatriz de Salcedo**, a widowed distant cousin of Isabel's, accompanying María José at her mother's request. Both women are in black, María José in mourning for Juan.
- **The decoy** is **Inés Barreda**, an Altamira seamstress who runs messages through Old Pico's stable. She's close to the fugitive's height and build, which is why she volunteered. She's brave but no fighter, and she knows it. Dressed as a young man and answering to "Rodrigo," she will ride with Rafi and Lute. Inés knows Pico's stable is a drop, two Altamira safehouses, the couriers' names, and that Las Robles shelters people. She knows nothing about María José's half of the plan.
- **The bodyguard.** After some table debate (a carriage was proposed and dropped in favor of horses, and Cristóbal was considered and passed over while he's in this state), the job goes to **Sancho**, a stable hand who lives in the bunkhouse and is some sort of very distant cousin. Level-headed, steady, armed with a musket.
- **Marching order.** Rafi, Lute and "Rodrigo" ride in front, with María José's party about a hundred yards behind, close enough to shout, far enough apart not to look like one group. If the Inquisition stops anyone, it should be the boys. Rafi's plan for that is to play the outraged rich man's son and make a scene loud enough for the ladies to roll quietly past.

That evening there's an informal wake in the dining hall. Nobody talks business, only stories about Juan as a boy. Ana keeps the kitchen going, the mezcal and small beer go around, and Lute raids the platters with no regard for what happened last time.


## The Checkpoint

Julius 6, toward dusk. The road into Altamira is choked with the whole countryside coming in for Los Borrachos: carts of wine casks, children on shoulders, a man leading a goat with ribbons on its horns. Where the road crosses the old stone bridge, a wagon has been pulled across half the span. Torches burn on poles, and between them men in the black and red of the Holy Inquisition stop everyone who passes. Someone sets off a firework early over the city. It bursts green, and half the line cheers.

There are two churchmen at the table: a bored older sergeant who would rather be at the festival, and a young clerk named Fidel with a little notebook. Six bored guards with halberds and pistols stand around.

The sergeant sighs at the boys. "Amigo. Amigo. And amigo." Is the cousin going to be sick on his bridge? "Rodrigo" stumbles convincingly with a wine jug. Fidel looks Lute over and says what nobody can miss: "You are not Castillian, señor. Eisen? And the iron?" Into the notebook it goes. Then Fidel takes the drunk cousin's chin, turns the face toward the torchlight and asks the name. "Rodrigo," says Inés.

That's when Rafi discovers, very loudly, that his dearest friend has been lying to him for years about being Castillian. He knocks Lute off his horse. Lute dodges badly. Rafi swings at him again, then backs his horse into him for good measure. "This is between me and my brother!" It turns into sobbing, shoving, barrels, screaming and a Tom-and-Jerry brawl rolling across the bridge with half the line watching. Fidel sits back down with a little smile. The only problem is that they're blocking the bridge, so the feud has to move along into town, loudly and slowly.

Then the ladies reach the table. "Señora. Señora. Where to tonight?" To visit Captain Ocaña, señor. María José Torres y Soldano. The sergeant knows the name: Las Robles, his sympathies for her brother, a terrible thing. María José introduces Doña Beatriz de Salcedo, a relation of her mother's, and Sancho, one of the men of the hacienda and her protector for the evening.

Fidel steps around the table. "Uno momento, Sargento. Forgive me, señora." He asks the duena her name, and she answers for herself. Then: would she lift her veil, only for a moment? They have been asked to look at every face tonight.

She begins to lift it, eyes downcast, and María José handles the rest. A widow's grief is not for the road. The sergeant has had enough. "Hermano. It is a widow and a child from a funeral. Let them through." Fidel bows, apologizes and goes back to his table. María José thanks them both for their understanding and graciousness.

As they pass between the torches, the duena's hand finds María José's under the edge of her shawl. It's shaking. It doesn't stop shaking until the bridge is well behind them.

María José glances back once and sees what nobody else does. Fidel is writing in his notebook. He tears out the page, hands it to a boy, and the boy sprints across the bridge into the city.

(Rafi and Lute each earned a Drama Die for the brawl.)


## The Warehouse Street

The boys loop through town and come back toward the docks from the north. The street narrows, warehouses press close, and crates are stacked three and four high, some spilling straw across the cobbles. A cargo net full of barrels sways from a hoist overhead, there are coils of rope outside a ship's chandlery, and a rickety rope-and-plank gangway runs between two warehouse roofs. Over the city the first real fireworks go up.

They're passing under the gangway when the pistols go off from behind the crates. Rafi takes a ball (15 Flesh Wounds) and so does Lute, twice (30). Lute takes a Dramatic Wound to clear them. Then an eight-man Brute Squad dressed in Inquisition colors steps out from behind the barrels on either side of them, and there are more beyond those. Thirty-two Inquisitors are waiting in this street.

Rafi moves first. He stands up in the saddle, leaps for the gangway, catches it and flips himself up onto the planks. He draws a pistol and fires down into the men who still have loaded guns: three brutes drop to one shot (grapeshot, the table decided). Then he somersaults off the gangway onto the crates beside the nearest squad and comes down swinging from above them, taking out four more. That's a squad gone.

That's when they see the jewel. A henchman climbs onto a double stack of crates, and something glows red at his throat. He shoots Rafi (15 more) and misses Lute. The brutes close in four to a man. Rafi dodges one blade and takes the rest: 33 Flesh Wounds, a failed Wound Check, and two Dramatic Wounds. Lute shrugs off 24 and goes to work with the Panzerhand, poking eyes, raking claws and ripping through all four men in front of him. By the end of the round the two of them have put down twelve of the thirty-two.

The man with the jewel leaps down off the crates, stabs Lute and snatches up "Rodrigo," throws Inés over his shoulder and runs.

Rafi has first action in the next round. "Unhand my seamstress!" At long range he puts a pistol ball into the running man's leg, nearly at the knee, and the man goes down hard with two Dramatic Wounds. Rafi closes in and cuts him again for good measure. The henchman starts to crawl away, bleeding. The other henchman goes for Lute and lands a hit that tips him past his limit: three Dramatic Wounds, one for each point of Resolve, and no more exploding dice. With both their leaders down, an entire squad of eight melts back into the dark.

Lute scoops up Inés and starts for the docks, shouting for his ship the whole way. Then he remembers the red jewel, still around the neck of the man crawling away. He drops the seamstress, turns back, and rakes the Panzerhand across the crawling man's skull until his claws catch the chain, then tears it free. One more blow leaves the man two wounds from death and out of the fight. Rafi trades sword cuts with the second henchman, who is still on his feet.


## Meanwhile, at the Ship

María José, Doña Beatriz and Sancho heard the first shots and turned off a couple of streets early, staying out of the fight and on the ordinary way down to the docks. They reached Captain Ocaña's ship and went aboard, and the swap was made. The fugitive stayed with the ship.

María José wasn't about to stand on deck listening to gunfire. She sent Sancho off with his musket to scout, then went back down the gangplank herself with the captain's wife, now wearing Beatriz's mantilla, since two women are safer than one on a night like this.

On the way she passes a very odd-looking ship with red-and-white striped sails and what appears to be a carnival under way on deck: acrobats, a man doing magic tricks. It's Lute's ship. Three sailors coming down the gangplank hail her in Montaigne, asking about the gunshots. María José answers in Montaigne and asks who's in charge. The half-mate points to Gomez, a very old Castillian sailor with a long grey beard and a ragged peacoat, standing at the top of the gangplank.

"Señor, your Captain Lute is in trouble. There are a dozen men against him." Gomez holds up a hand, points at the sailors already on the gangplank and sends them, and six more grab cutlasses and pistols and follow her at a run.

They come in from the dockside, behind the fight. Eight pistols go off at once, and the last of the brutes go down.


## Where We Left It

The street is full of smoke and bodies. Rafi and Lute are standing, but barely: two Dramatic Wounds for Rafi and three for Lute. Lute's crew is spilling into the street. Inés is alive and still free. One henchman is wounded and the other lies at Lute's feet, ankles shattered and scalp torn, inches from death. And Lute's iron fist is closed around a blood-red jewel that glows.

We stopped mid-scene.


## Loose Threads

- **The jewel.** Lute tore a glowing red stone off a henchman's neck. What is it, and why was an Inquisition officer wearing it?
- **Fidel's notebook.** It has an Eisen sailor with an iron hand in it, a Castillian sailor with him, and a drunk cousin named "Rodrigo." The young clerk also tore out a page and sent a boy running into the city right before the ambush. María José saw it.
- **Two henchmen down, alive.** One is wounded and one is dying, and both are still in the street. What happens to them, and what do they know?
- **Inés.** She knows Old Pico's drop, two safehouses and that Las Robles shelters people. She can't stay in Altamira now. Can she get aboard before the tide?
- **The fugitive.** She's aboard Captain Ocaña's ship. Does the ship make the night tide with everything that's going on at the docks?
- **The ones who got away.** A whole squad of eight slipped off into the dark, and they saw Rafi and Lute's faces.
- **The wounds.** Rafi and Lute are both badly hurt, and Lute has no exploding dice left until he heals.
- **Gomez and the crew.** Lute's crew now knows its captain is mixed up in something with the Inquisition.
